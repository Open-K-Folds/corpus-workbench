# Bounded token restructuring

Source **0.2.0-dev.3** adds native, previewed split/merge and a usable authoring form for a closed TEITOK text dialect. This advances B19/B20; arbitrary converters, deletion and full dialect parity remain gates. The published `0.1.0-preview.1` and existing sessions remain separate and immutable.

## Supported transformation

One operation group splits one plain untimed token into two, or merges two ordered adjacent sibling tokens into one. Split successors are byte-adjacent. A merge separator may be empty or at most 64 literal ASCII spaces; its exact text becomes part of every present merged layer. Other whitespace/markup requires a converter. Original token body must equal decoded `form` when supplied. Original, corrected and normalized layers conserve their content independently, including Unicode code points and nullable layer presence. No normalization, alignment or word timestamps are inferred. Explicit variety and normalized-review provenance must agree for a merge.

The whole package must satisfy `teitok-plain-untimed/1`: unqualified `TEI/text/body/u/tok`, an empty `Resources/settings.xml`, uniquely associated `spanGrp/span` files, and strictly typed immutable workbench lineage or verified export history. The carrier rules are shown per artifact in the proof. Unknown attributes/elements/namespaces, comments/PI/CDATA, schema/configuration expressions, scripts, indexes, opaque media and raw-draft formats **block** execution even when they do not mention a selected ID. Their original bytes are retained. The existing richer correction and annotation editors continue to preserve advanced packages; this structural dialect does not claim to decode them.

Splitting a whole-token span replaces that occurrence with both successors in order. Merging a whole-token span requires both old tokens consecutively in that same order; a partial or reordered span is blocked. Character anchors move only when their exact corrected-layer quote fits one successor; merge offsets include the proven separator. Crossing split anchors require a later representation/converter. Incoming directed relations on a split require an explicit successor choice. Selected outgoing relations or authored notes are blocked because this representation cannot safely redistribute them. Literal ID-like text remains literal.

## Revision and identity contract

`POST /api/retokenize-preview` and `retokenize-preview --request FILE` require schema 1, project, exact revision/snapshot/config/inventory hashes, document-qualified byte-position targets, replacement readings and an optional explicit relation endpoint. The result provides a preview hash, blockers, mappings, candidate XML, per-artifact rules and prospective snapshot hash. Preview writes no authority objects. A `retokenize` command must carry that exact request and preview hash as its sole operation; the native authority regenerates the proof and rechecks the head inside the existing transactional commit.

Each committed transformation adds a hash-named immutable `Resources/retokenization/<SHA256>.json` record with the exact request, successor mapping and artifact hashes. Current records, native history and verified exported historical objects reserve retired and created IDs. Undo/restore retain intervening history; a copied package carrying the lineage record cannot reuse retired IDs. Complete connected parent history and exact exported-row equality are required for managed exports. Missing ancestors, detached rows, extra namespace artifacts or unknown lineage fields are rejected. Removing provenance externally is not a supported reconciliation workflow.

Approval remains attached to its exact revision. Structural changes require new review and stale prior derived generations. Historical V1/V2 compiler identities, receipts, graphs and contracts retain their original bytes and scope. Structural history diffs expose token additions/retirements, qualified span repairs and immutable mapping records. No parallel ledger is introduced.

## Authoring and verification

The **Split / merge** tab partitions each layer independently, asks for fresh IDs and relation choices, and exposes the candidate XML before enabling commit. Edits invalidate a pending preview. Structural drafts survive navigation and prevent competing annotation/token commits. Stale commits preserve the draft and require reload plus a fresh proof. Review requires existing drafts to be resolved and records its captured exact revision, even when another tab advances the head. Authoring controls lock for the awaited save or review, preventing a new draft from being erased when its response arrives. A delayed preview cannot authorize a subsequently edited draft.

To prepare a separate synthetic text package, run `python scripts/prepare-structural-synthetic.py --out NEW_PROTECTED_DIRECTORY`, import it into a new protected authority and use the existing loopback launcher pattern. The normal audio/ASR synthetic package intentionally remains outside this structural dialect. The new fixture is synthetic text only.

Local validation covers **114 Rust tests**: 36 core contracts, 19 annotation edits, six export integrity, ten generation contracts, three earlier process-crash tests, 15 inventory tests, 22 structural tests and three review regressions. The structural tests include unknown-carrier falsification, Unicode/entity conservation, character/discontinuous coverage, absent layers, stale/forged proofs, ID reservation after restore/copy/reimport, incomplete exported ancestry, complete repeated export/reimport, backup, three abrupt structural process exits and deterministic overlapping-retry barriers after lookup and before structural proof, including different-payload rejection.

**Twelve actual browser flows** include the eight earlier flows and four structural journeys: proved split/merge, exact review invalidation/diff/undo/redo/restore/export/reopen; preserved competing drafts and stale/delayed proofs; blocked unknown carrier and an authenticated foreign-project denial; draft-safe review with an exact reviewed-revision message across a delayed response. Desktop and 390-pixel layouts are checked. TypeScript/Vite, Rustfmt and strict Clippy run; hosted CI checks the exact proposed commit on Windows x86-64, Linux x86-64 and Linux ARM64 plus Linux browser QA before integration.

Optional installed-native Semantica 0.6.8 verification adds three structural generations (7 nodes/13 edges before, 8/16 after split, 7/13 after merge) to the six prior scenarios. Retired graph objects remain unchanged; unreviewed dispatch is rejected and word timings stay absent. This locally supplied runtime is not installed by public CI.

The complete synthetic split export also passed the retained pinned TEITOK PHP 8.3.6 SimpleXML/XPath helpers: four tokens, two spans, resolved endpoints, separate readings and the Unicode anchor. This ran in a temporary read-only, networkless container using source `57d864b57fa3ed9f0a660a7781533ce7fa09ae2b`; no existing trial/project was restarted or modified. Full TEITOK browser reopening of this new structural export has not run.

Independent review reproduced and repaired five preservation/currentness defects before publication: retired IDs in copied lineage, omitted exported ancestors, drafts edited during a delayed save, dirty drafts cleared by review refresh, and overlapping identical commands rejected before their committed binding was rechecked. Permanent falsification covers each.

## Remaining gates

Broader TEITOK settings/header/schema/media/raw-ASR carrier decoders; mixed/nested tokens and arbitrary references; explicit linguistic judgment redistribution; partial-coverage merge converters and multi-segment character anchors; timed restructuring/deletion; CWB/CQP index rebuild/parity; external-edit reconciliation; durable jobs/outbox/worker fencing; rights withdrawal and production roles/backups; human linguistic review and physical Pi validation. The complete approved migration is unfinished. No new release, persistent demo, production or hardware deployment is part of this milestone.
